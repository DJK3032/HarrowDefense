Harrow Defense builds body-worn electronic systems for people who work beyond reliable infrastructure. We founded the company in early 2026 around one product, PYLON, and one conviction: the sensors already on a soldier should reach the network, and every soldier should have a position on it. We took PYLON from concept to assembled prototype hardware without outside capital or a government award, and the intellectual property is founder-developed and company-owned.

How We Work

Lean by design. Specialist partners where it counts. IP in-house.

Harrow keeps non-recurring engineering low by owning its intellectual property and pairing founder-led product architecture with specialist U.S. partners: printed circuit board design and assembly by a U.S. electrical-engineering firm, enclosure design and additive manufacturing domestically, and compliance handled in-house. The result is a company that reached assembled prototype hardware for a fraction of what a conventional defense hardware startup spends to get there.

Path to Fielding

A named transition customer and engagement across the offices that own the problem.

Primary transition customer
Portfolio Acquisition Executive Maneuver Ground (PAE Maneuver Ground), the Army acquisition portfolio that owns soldier systems and Soldier Borne Mission Command. PYLON is the missing transport layer the Army's adaptive squad architecture defines.
Research and standards
Army DEVCOM C5ISR Center, which sets interoperability standards for soldier-borne sensors and leads electronic-warfare and sensor research. PYLON is the physical transport layer those standards specify. White papers submitted and under evaluation.
Marine Corps
Capability brief delivered to the Marine Corps Warfighting Laboratory in August 2026.
Special operations
Army special operations and Army modernization solicitations have identified the low-probability-of-intercept position mesh and the warfighter sensor bridge as capability gaps. PYLON maps to both.
Vehicles
